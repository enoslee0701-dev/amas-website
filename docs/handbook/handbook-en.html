1. Welcome to AMAS Chiang Mai

Welcome to a journey of knowing the Scriptures systematically, building your life, and preparing to serve.

AMAS Chiang Mai is rooted in the truth of Scripture with an evangelical theological foundation. We value systematic study — and equally, truth put into practice in real life. We long to help learners gradually grow in reading, discernment, expression, shepherding, serving, discipling others and passing on the gospel.

Theology is not merely knowing more; it is knowing God more faithfully, following Christ, and serving people.

The Chiang Mai Learning Centre builds on online study communities as an important foundation, combined with in-person participation and licensed lecture recordings for catch-up, so that those juggling work, family or church service can keep moving forward.

2. Our Vision and Educational Direction

Faithful to Scripture

Testing theology, teaching and practice by the Bible; holding fast the core truth of the gospel.

Building Lives

Learning that brings repentance, obedience, character growth and renewed relationships.

Connected to the Church

The seminary does not replace the local church; it helps learners serve their churches better.

Responding to Mission

Equipping preachers, church co-workers, mission workers and believers willing to serve faithfully.

Practice-Oriented

Bringing classroom learning into family, church, relationships and real ministry settings.

Sustainable Learning

Online, in-person and flexible catch-up combined, helping learners start, persevere and finish.

3. Who Should Apply

  • Believers willing to know the Scriptures and the Christian faith systematically;
  • Those who want a stable foundation of faith and are committed to continued, serious study;
  • Those already serving in a church who hope to be equipped more systematically;
  • Those preparing to take up teaching, small-group, care, preaching, discipleship or mission responsibilities;
  • Those who cannot enter traditional full-time theological education because of work, family, distance or time — but can commit stable study time.
In reviewing applications the seminary looks at whether the applicant truly understands the programme, is willing to commit stable time, and is willing to accept the shared shaping of truth, life and ministry practice. Specific entry conditions and quotas are subject to the notice current at the time.

7. How We Learn, and a Learner's Responsibilities

Online learning community

Learning within real relationships — discussion, care, prayer and walking together.

Online participation

Joining online teaching, reading, discussion and assignments as each course is arranged.

Licensed recordings for catch-up

Provided within the permission of the teacher and course team; not all courses are recorded.

Life and ministry practice

Applying what is learned in family, church, workplace, relationships and gospel action.

Every learner should prepare

  • Stable weekly study time
  • A device and internet access adequate for the courses
  • Reading, assignments and assessments completed as required
  • Proactive communication about absence or difficulty
  • Respect for teachers, classmates and church relationships
  • Confidentiality of what is shared in class and pastoral settings

8. Fees, Giving and Learning Support

The seminary sets course fees on the principle that they should be affordable and keep the work sustainable. The official fee schedule, payment timing, reductions, scholarships and credit-retention arrangements are subject to written notice at the time; until fees are formally confirmed, no individual's verbal promise may be treated as a fee basis.

Tuition and voluntary giving are kept strictly separate. Giving is voluntary and has no bearing on application, admission, grades, credits or graduation. Those in genuine financial difficulty who are committed to serious study may apply through the seminary's process for reduction, instalments or other learning support; personal financial circumstances are handled confidentially by designated staff.

9. Application and Enrolment Process

  1. Initial enquiryLearn about the seminary's direction, the two pathways, how courses run and current openings.
  2. Choose a pathwayConfirm whether you are applying for formal B.Th student status or joining companion learning.
  3. Submit materialsComplete the application form as required at the time, with the necessary identity, faith, church and study information.
  4. Conversation and reviewAdmissions staff confirm motivation, study time, understanding of the pathway and matters needing coordination; whether an interview is needed follows current requirements.
  5. Written outcomeThe outcome may be formal admission, acceptance into companion learning, a request for further materials, a suggestion to defer, or non-admission.
  6. Registration and orientationComplete registration, payment and rules confirmation as notified, join the orientation session, then begin study.

10. Student Status, Graduation and Conferral

  • Formal B.Th student status is established through the application, review and registration procedures of headquarters and the seminary.
  • Graduation review is based on the formal course plan, completed course records, assessment results and the finally confirmed credit system.
  • Companion-learning records do not convert automatically into degree credits or graduation qualification.
  • Practical trainings may enter a learner's growth record, but are not a hidden graduation requirement.
  • Credit transfer, exemption, make-up, retakes, leave, resumption, graduation review, certificate signing and conferral follow the formal system as issued.

This handbook makes no promises regarding educational accreditation, degree equivalence, employment, immigration, visas or cross-border recognition. Such matters are subject only to formal documents issued by the competent authorities.

11. Information and Privacy

Application materials, faith background, church relationships, contact details, payment information and pastoral conversations are used only for necessary admissions, service, learning support and secure administration. Both the seminary and learners should respect others' privacy; nothing may be made public, forwarded or used for unrelated publicity without permission.
